Short guides to the terms, trade-offs and practical questions that shape a South African solar decision.

01 · Energy basics

kW, kWh and kWp

kW measures power at a moment; kWh measures energy over time; kWp is the rated peak capacity of a solar panel array under standard test conditions.

02 · Components

Panels, inverters and batteries

Panels generate direct-current electricity. An inverter converts it for household use. A battery stores energy; its usable capacity and power limit both matter.

03 · Weather and seasons

Cloudy days and winter

Cloud reduces output; shorter winter days and changing sun angle affect yield. A typical annual estimate does not mean each day will produce the same amount.

04 · Buying well

Compare quotations

Ask for itemized equipment, design assumptions, warranties, installation scope, compliance documents, grid approvals and a clear statement of expected performance.

05 · Compliance

Safety and approvals

Electrical work must meet applicable South African standards and be certified by an appropriately registered person. Grid connection and embedded-generation rules can be municipality-specific.

06 · Long-term care

Degradation and maintenance

Panels gradually lose output; inverters and batteries may have different useful lives. Review warranty exclusions, monitoring, cleaning advice and replacement costs.

Authoritative guidance

Electrical and municipal requirements change. Consult the current SANS 10142-1 materials, NRS 097 requirements where applicable, the local municipality's embedded-generation rules, and a registered electrical contractor. This guide is educational, not legal or electrical advice.
